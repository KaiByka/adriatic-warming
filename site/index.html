How to read it

Columns are years, 1960 to 2025. Rows are months, January at the top, December at the bottom. The color of each cell is that month's temperature measured against the 1991–2020 average for the same month.

The three blocks are the three basins of the Adriatic: the shallow north behind the islands, the long central channel, the deep south. The shallow north has less water to warm, so it answers the sun first — it also answers climate change first.

Data & method — full disclosure

What this is. Monthly mean sea-surface temperature on a 1°×1° grid, measured by ships and buoys — the ICOADS record, which begins in 1960, decades before the satellite era. Dataset: … , NOAA ICOADS via NOAA PSL's public ERDDAP server.

Area. Adriatic box, lat 40.5–45.5 °N, lon 12.5–19.5 °E: … grid cells, of which … report sea values — north …, central …, south …. Sub-basin split: north ≥ 43.6 °N, central 41.8–43.6 °N, south < 41.8 °N.

Processing. Each region-month is the mean of that region's sea cells for that month. Each cell's color is its anomaly: region-month mean minus the 1991–2020 mean for the same month (baseline: …). Displayed precision: temperatures 0.1 °C, anomalies 0.01 °C.

Missing data. A month with no observations is shown as "no data", never as zero. In the loaded series: north … missing months (longest gap …), central … (longest gap …), south … (longest gap …) — computed live from the data file below.

Honesty note. Ship sampling is sparse in some winter coastal cells; the gaps above are real gaps in the observational record, not processing errors. Every number on this page is an area mean over a sub-basin's sea cells, not a point measurement.
