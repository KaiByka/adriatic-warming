Key facts

  • Summer (Jun–Aug) warming, 1960–2025: North Adriatic +0.28 °C/decade (+1.80 °C over the whole period), Central +0.26 °C/decade (+1.68 °C), South +0.30 °C/decade (+1.93 °C). Annual trends: +0.14 / +0.18 / +0.18 °C/decade.
  • Warmest years in this series (annual anomaly vs 1991–2020): North 2015 (+1.19 °C), Central 2012 (+0.90 °C), South 2015 (+0.82 °C).
  • Data: NOAA ICOADS 1-degree Enhanced monthly sea-surface temperature (public domain), measured by ships and buoys on a 1° grid — 35 sea cells in the Adriatic box: north 7, central 13, south 15. Baseline: 1991–2020 monthly means. Fetched 2026-11-20; exact source query in Data & method.
  • Missing months (shown dark, never as zero): north 17 of 792 (longest gap 2 months), central 2 of 792, south 1 of 792.

How to read it

Columns are years, 1960 to 2025. Rows are months, January at the top, December at the bottom. The color of each cell is that month's temperature measured against the 1991–2020 average for the same month.

The three blocks are the three basins of the Adriatic: the shallow north behind the islands, the long central channel, the deep south. In this series all three basins show a warming trend.

Hover or tap any cell to see the measured value. Hovering one year lights up the same column in every block, so you can compare the basins year by year. Click a cell to pin it; shift-click a year bar to compare two years.

Processing. Each region-month is the mean of that region's sea cells for that month. Each cell's color is its anomaly: region-month mean minus the 1991–2020 mean for the same month (baseline: …). Displayed precision: temperatures 0.1 °C, anomalies 0.01 °C.

Honesty note. Ship sampling is sparse in some winter coastal cells; the gaps above are real gaps in the observations, not processing errors. Every number on this page is an area mean over a sub-basin's sea cells, not a point measurement.

Questions people ask

Is the Adriatic Sea getting warmer?

Yes. In the ship-measured series on this page (1960–2025) all three basins warm: summer (Jun–Aug) trends are +0.28 °C/decade (north), +0.26 (central) and +0.30 (south); annual trends +0.14 to +0.18 °C/decade — all against the 1991–2020 baseline.

Which part of the Adriatic warms most?

The South Adriatic shows the strongest summer trend (+0.30 °C/decade); the North Adriatic the weakest annual trend (+0.14 °C/decade). Every figure on this page is computed from the loaded data, not asserted.

Where does the data come from?

NOAA ICOADS 1-degree Enhanced (sst.mean), ERDDAP dataset noaa_psl_3a7b_f45b_d09a on NOAA PSL's public server upwell.pfeg.noaa.gov — public domain, fetched 2026-11-20. The exact query URL, license and processing steps are in Data & method.

What does a cell's color mean?

Columns are years (1960–2025), rows are months (January at the top). A cell's color is that month's temperature anomaly versus the 1991–2020 average for the same month: blue colder, red warmer.

Are some months missing?

Yes — ship coverage is sparse in some winter months: north 17 of 792 (longest gap 2 months), central 2 of 792, south 1 of 792. Missing months are shown dark and read "no data", never as zero.

Can journalists reuse this figure?

Yes — the data is public domain (NOAA ICOADS, a US Government work). Please credit "NOAA ICOADS 1-degree Enhanced via NOAA PSL ERDDAP" with the query URL above, and link this page. The pipeline (make data) is public and reproducible.
